Many investors rule out a building as soon as they see it is listed. It is a common mistake: protection does not prevent transformation, it only **defines what can be touched and how**. Well managed, the building's character becomes a value no new building can offer.

## 1. Know exactly what is protected

The first step is reading the catalogue entry. Not all listed buildings have the same level of protection, and the entry specifies which elements are protected: the façade, the volume, the roof, interior features such as stairs, ceilings or floors, or the garden.

What is not protected can be transformed with much more freedom. That is why you need to know it **before buying**, not when design starts.

## 2. Check the condition of what is protected

Restoring a façade, original joinery or encaustic tile floors has a cost that must be budgeted from the start. In due diligence we review the condition of each protected element and estimate its restoration.

## 3. Request a pre-consultation

In Barcelona, works on catalogued buildings may require a **prior heritage report**, which can be favourable, favourable with conditions or unfavourable. Before developing the design, we recommend a pre-consultation: presenting the proposed works and learning the council's criteria.

A well-prepared consultation saves months: it avoids designing something that will not be approved and lets conditions be built in from the start.

## 4. Design with heritage on your side

The best design for a listed building does not hide what is protected: it showcases it. Restored ceilings, mouldings, stained glass or floors are what give the result its **distinctive character** and make a space unique against the competition.

The key is to deliver the new programme (services, accessibility, fire safety) without damaging what is protected: routing services out of sight, integrating lifts without altering the original stair, keeping interventions reversible.

## 5. Coordinate construction and restoration

Works on a listed building require restoration specialists and site supervision that knows the heritage criteria. Any change during construction that affects protected elements may need a new consultation, so it pays to define everything at design stage.

## Three examples

- **IESE Business School, Building L.** A listed 1910 building transformed into offices with a favourable heritage report. Façade, roof, timber and floors restored.
- **Fundació Familiar Catalana.** Special plan and transformation of a listed building into offices for UIC Barcelona.
- **Associació Col·laborativa Montroig.** Full transformation of a senior residence in a listed building, with the residence fully operational.

## In short

A listed building is not a problem; it is a starting condition. Knowing what is protected, what condition it is in and what the heritage criteria will be before buying turns risk into advantage. It is our speciality: we help you assess it before investing and transform it afterwards.
