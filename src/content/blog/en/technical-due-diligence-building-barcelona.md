Buying a building to transform it is a bet on what it **could become**, not on what it is today. Technical due diligence tests whether that bet stands up: what planning allows, what heritage protects, what condition the building is in and what it will cost to bring it to the use you have in mind.

At DTRES we frame it around one final question: **buy, negotiate or walk away?** Everything else serves that answer.

## 1. Planning status: what can be done

This is the first filter and the one that rules out most deals. You need to know:

- **Zoning and permitted uses.** The zoning code in the Barcelona Metropolitan General Plan (PGM), and any detailed plan affecting the plot, determines whether your intended use (residential, residence, offices, hotel) is possible.
- **Buildable area and volume.** If the building exceeds what current planning allows, it may be *non-conforming*, which limits the works you can carry out.
- **Housing density.** In Barcelona the maximum number of homes per plot is capped. In a recent study for a fund, the plot allowed 39 homes and already had 31: the real margin was 8, not however many would physically fit.
- **Use-specific regulations.** Special plans restricting certain activities in some neighbourhoods, or the affordable-housing reserve on larger schemes, can change the return completely.

The starting document is the **municipal planning report or certificate**, but reading it is not enough: it has to be interpreted for the specific use you have in mind.

## 2. Heritage protection

Many of Barcelona's buildings with the most potential are **listed**. That does not prevent transforming them, but it defines what can be touched. Check:

- The **protection level** in the municipal catalogue and which elements it protects (façade, volume, interior features, garden).
- Whether the works will need a **heritage report** and how much time that adds.
- The condition of the protected elements, because restoring them has a cost that must be budgeted from the start.

At IESE's Building L, a listed 1910 building, the heritage pre-consultation is what allowed the change of use to offices to be planned with confidence before design began.

## 3. Technical condition

Here the building is assessed through a contractor's eyes:

- **Structure:** type, visible defects, timber floors or joists that may need opening-up works.
- **Façades and roof:** watertightness, loose elements, condition of windows and doors.
- **Services:** almost always fully replaced in a change of use; what matters is whether there is room to do it (light wells, risers, roof).
- **Existing documents:** building technical inspection (ITE) or assessment report, energy certificate, previous permits.

If there are reasonable doubts, we recommend **targeted opening-up or testing** before signing. It costs little compared with a structural surprise mid-construction.

## 4. Habitability, accessibility and fire safety for the future use

A building may be fine for what it is today and fail for what you want it to be. For the **future use**, check:

- Habitability standards (for homes, Catalan Decree 141/2012): floor areas, ceiling heights, ventilation and daylight in each room.
- Accessibility: whether a lift fits, and where.
- Fire safety: escape routes, protected stairs, compartmentation.

These three points are what usually move the number of units, and therefore the value.

## 5. Cost, timeline and return

Due diligence ends in numbers:

- **Estimated budget** for the transformation, as a range.
- **Realistic timeline** for permits and works, including heritage where relevant.
- **Product scenarios:** how many units, what size and what use.

With this, an investor can compare the asking price with the end value and the cost of getting there.

## Documents to request from the seller

- Land registry extract and title deeds.
- Previous drawings and permits, habitability certificates or activity licences.
- ITE or building assessment report, and energy certificate.
- Where possible, full access to roofs, basements and light wells.

## In short

Good due diligence is not a long report; it is a **well-founded decision**: what the building allows, what it costs and how long it takes. If you are assessing a building in Barcelona or Catalonia, we can help you get there before you commit.
